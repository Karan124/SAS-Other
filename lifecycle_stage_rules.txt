# Customer-to-Institution

## Overview

A layered control model across the payment lifecycle, with stage-specific controls addressing risks inherent at each point of the process, system-wide controls applied consistently across the lifecycle, and governance controls acting as an enabler.

---

## Payment Lifecycle

### 1. Initiation, Validation & Authorisation

**Description**
- Customer submits a repayment or deposit via ATM, branch, or digital channel.
- Validate account, reference, account matching, and cut-off checks.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT1:** Validation of the correctness of transaction details
- **CT6:** Master data input validation

---

### 2. Execution & Early Processing Assurance

**Description**
- Receipt of funds via the relevant channel.
- Highlight and identify exceptions.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT3:** Identification of duplications and processing errors
- **CT4:** Payments interface / batch error detection

---

### 3. Clearing / Settlement

**Description**
- Interbank settlement or internal settlement.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT14:** Logging and monitoring

**Note**
- Risk mitigation is expected to occur upstream.

---

### 4. Posting, Accounting & Detection

**Description**
- Apply funds to loan, card or deposit account.
- Perform reconciliations.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT2:** Error Detection
- **CT27:** Records retention

**Note**
- This stage is primarily controlled via upstream validation activities.

---

### 5. Notification & Reporting

**Description**
- Customer confirmations.
- Balance updates.
- Statements.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT22:** Mistaken Internet Payment Reports
- **CT23:** Provision of Confirmations and Notifications

---

### 6. Incident Response, Disputes, Recovery Follow-ups

**Description**
- Investigate mis-posting.
- Resolve suspense items.
- Process adjustments and recoveries.

**Stage-Specific Controls**
- **CT5:** Incident response
- **CT24:** Treatment of Unauthorised Payments

---

# Large Reasoning Model Interpretation

## Lifecycle Stages

1. Initiation, Validation & Authorisation
2. Execution & Early Processing Assurance
3. Clearing / Settlement
4. Posting, Accounting & Detection
5. Notification & Reporting
6. Incident Response, Disputes, Recovery Follow-ups

## Control Architecture

The operating model consists of three layers:

### Layer 1: Process / Stage-Specific Controls
Controls designed to address risks within a specific payment lifecycle stage.

Examples:
- CT1, CT2, CT3, CT4, CT5, CT6
- CT22, CT23, CT24, CT27
- CT14 (stage-specific monitoring)

### Layer 2: System / Third-Party Controls
Controls that provide technology, security, operational resilience and third-party risk management across all lifecycle stages.

Examples:
- Secure IT Design
- Vulnerability Management
- Privileged Access Management
- Patch Management
- Access Monitoring
- System Recovery Capability
- Logging & Monitoring
- Material Service Provider Ongoing Review

### Layer 3: Governance Controls
Enterprise-wide controls responsible for:
- Change management
- Operational resilience
- Governance oversight
- Third-party governance

These controls act as enablers and provide overarching risk management across the entire Customer-to-Institution payment process.

## Key Design Principle

For each payment lifecycle stage:

Risk Mitigation = Stage-Specific Controls
                + System Controls
                + Third-Party Controls
                + Governance Controls

Where:
- Stage-specific controls address process execution risk.
- System controls address technology and security risk.
- Third-party controls address external dependency risk.
- Governance controls address oversight, resilience and change risk.
