2. ML Techniques for Text Classification — And Why LLM Wins Here
Best traditional ML techniques for text classification:
TF-IDF + Support Vector Machine (SVM)
The strongest traditional baseline. Converts text to word frequency vectors, SVM finds the optimal boundary between classes. Fast, interpretable, well-understood.
BERT fine-tuning
Technically a neural/transformer approach but trained as a classifier rather than a generative model. Pre-trained on large text corpus, fine-tuned on your labelled examples. Best accuracy of any ML approach for text.
Why someone picks ML over LLM:

Deterministic — same input always produces same output. Critical for audit reproducibility
Speed — milliseconds per classification vs seconds for LLM
Cost at scale — no per-token pricing, runs on local compute
No hallucination risk — produces a class label and probability, nothing invented
Versioned model artefact — frozen, deployable, testable

Why ML loses for your specific use case:

The decisive point: You have 28 CT outcomes × 7 lifecycle stages × 5 Payment Natures. To train a reliable ML classifier you'd need thousands of labelled examples across hundreds of combinations you don't have. You'd spend more time labelling training data than the PoC itself takes to run. And the Neo4j team's own system uses an LLM for exactly this classification — which is the strongest argument you have.
