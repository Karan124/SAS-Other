---

## 2. Brief Context

This is a Proof of Concept for the Non-Financial Risk (NFR) Analytics team at Westpac. The initiative uses Azure OpenAI to analyse internal control and process data across the end-to-end payments lifecycle. Structured data from JUNO, Holocentric, obligation registers, incidents, and issues is processed to identify gaps, duplicates, and misalignments in the payments control environment. The work is exploratory and iterative, running in a non-production environment with SME validation at each stage.

---

## 3. Objectives

- Identify and validate the population of payment-related controls and processes across internal systems
- Classify controls by payment type, lifecycle stage, and master control outcome (CT1–28)
- Detect duplicate or overlapping controls and identify best-of-breed candidates for rationalisation
- Identify gaps in control coverage across payment risk categories and lifecycle stages
- Extract and register bespoke controls identified from incident and issue remediation narratives
- Produce a data-driven control sufficiency baseline to support ongoing payments risk monitoring

## 4. Content Filters

**Input — Violence, Hate, Sexual, Self-harm:** Low
**Output — Violence, Hate, Sexual, Self-harm:** Low

**Justification:** All inputs and outputs are internal financial risk and control management text. There is no scenario in this use case where harmful content categories are relevant. Low filtering is appropriate and avoids unnecessary interference with legitimate regulatory and risk language.

---

## 5. Prompt Shields

**Recommended:** Annotate and Block

**Justification:** Although this is an internal use case, blocking indirect and jailbreak attacks is the right default for any regulated environment. Annotate-only provides visibility without protection — given the sensitivity of internal risk data, blocking is the more appropriate posture.

---

## 6. Embedding Model

**Recommended:** text-embedding-3-large

**Justification:** This PoC relies on embedding quality for semantic duplicate detection (mechanism similarity) and bespoke control matching across 3,743 controls. text-embedding-3-large provides 3,072-dimensional vectors and meaningfully better semantic discrimination than ada-002 or 3-small. Embeddings are computed once so the marginal cost difference is negligible.

---

## 7. Non-Production Completion Model Deployment Type

**Recommended:** Standard / Pay as you go

**Justification:** Usage is iterative, bursty, and unpredictable during PoC experimentation. Standard pricing aligns cost directly to consumption with no commitment risk. PTU is designed for stable, production-scale workloads with predictable throughput — that profile does not apply here.

---

## 8. Non-Prod PTU Justification

PTU is not required for this initiative. Usage is non-production and experimental in nature, consisting of prompt tuning, sample testing, and iterative batch-like analysis rather than sustained real-time serving. Standard pay-as-you-go provides sufficient capacity and avoids unnecessary committed spend for a PoC workload.

## 9. Non-Production Completion Usage Sizing

**Requests per minute:** 20

**Average input tokens per request:** 3,500

**Average output tokens per request:** 500

**Justification:** Usage is bursty rather than continuous — peak activity occurs during prompt testing and batch classification runs. 20 RPM with 3,500 input tokens reflects the expected pattern of processing full control descriptions (name, objective, description, test procedure) plus classification context. 500 output tokens covers structured JSON responses with rationale. These figures are conservative and realistic for a non-production PoC with SME-gated validation checkpoints between runs.

## 10. Non-Production Embeddings Model Usage

**Recommended estimate type:** Custom

**Tokens per minute:** 100,000
**Total minutes per month:** 200

**Justification:** Embedding computation is a one-time event in Sprint 2 covering ~3,743 controls at approximately 4,500 tokens each (~16.8M tokens total). At 100,000 TPM this completes in under 3 hours. 200 total minutes per month provides buffer for re-runs, prompt-related re-computation, and testing across the PoC sprint period without overstating ongoing need.
